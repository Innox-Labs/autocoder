CHIEF COMPLAINT
3-month follow-up of type 2 diabetes and hypertension; new bilateral foot numbness.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS
58-year-old established male presents for routine follow-up of type 2 diabetes
mellitus and essential hypertension. Home fasting glucose averaging 140-180 mg/dL.
Adherent to metformin. Reports 3 weeks of tingling and numbness in both feet, worse
at night. No ulcers or wounds. Home BP logs 138-145/85-92.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (dx 2016), essential hypertension, hyperlipidemia.

MEDICATIONS
Metformin 1000 mg BID, lisinopril 20 mg daily, atorvastatin 40 mg daily.

ALLERGIES
NKDA.

SOCIAL HISTORY
Former smoker, quit 2019. Occasional alcohol.

REVIEW OF SYSTEMS
Positive for bilateral foot paresthesia. Denies chest pain, dyspnea, polyuria,
vision changes, or foot wounds.

PHYSICAL EXAM
General: Well-appearing, no acute distress.
Cardiovascular: RRR, no murmurs.
Extremities: Diminished sensation to 10g monofilament bilaterally, decreased
vibratory sense at great toes. Dorsalis pedis pulses 2+. No ulcers, no erythema.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN
1. Type 2 diabetes mellitus with diabetic polyneuropathy - exam consistent with
   distal symmetric polyneuropathy. Order A1c and BMP. Start gabapentin 300 mg qHS.
   Continue metformin. Diabetic foot care counseling given.
2. Essential hypertension - not at goal on current therapy. Increase lisinopril to
   40 mg daily. Recheck in 4 weeks.
3. Hyperlipidemia - stable. Continue atorvastatin. Order lipid panel.
Return in 3 months or sooner if symptoms worsen.
